\begin{abstract}
As large language models (LLMs) ingest massive, uncurated web corpora during pretraining, they inherit not just factual knowledge but also the implicit vulnerabilities of their data sources. We uncover a novel and insidious threat vector: \textit{latent conceptual poisoning}, where adversaries embed \emph{warped, adversarially tilted concepts} into training data without relying on overt lexical patterns or duplications. We formalize this attack as \textbf{Stealth Pretraining Seeding (SPS)}—a subtle mechanism wherein maliciously crafted data implants \textbf{epistemic “landmines”} that remain dormant during typical evaluation but can be \emph{selectively detonated} via specific prompts. Crucially, these latent poisons distort the model’s internal \emph{belief geometry}, leading to \textbf{unsafe completions}, brittle reasoning, and \textbf{alignment drift}, all while evading detection by conventional token-based filters.

Drawing inspiration from biology—where \textit{silent genetic mutations} catalyze malignant cascades—we develop a mechanistic analogy for stealth seeding as \emph{epistemic mutagenesis}: a process that bypasses surface-level safeguards by corrupting latent concept representations. To evaluate this risk, we introduce \textbf{NEPHOS} (\textit{Neural Poisoning through Heuristic Overwrite and Seeding}), a benchmark suite spanning synthetic and real-world poisoning settings across diverse model families. We also propose \textit{spectral curvature analysis} and \textit{belief vector divergence} as diagnostic tools to detect such latent infections via their \emph{geometric and dynamic imprints}.

Our experiments reveal that even \textbf{minimal semantic infiltration} during pretraining can lead to \emph{profound alignment ruptures} during inference—compromising safety, reliability, and generalization. This work not only surfaces a previously underexplored \textbf{threat model} for foundation models, but also lays the groundwork for next-generation defenses centered on \textbf{latent space auditing}, \textbf{conceptual immunization}, and \textbf{proactive epistemic hygiene}.
\end{abstract}
