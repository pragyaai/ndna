\begin{abstract}
As large language models (LLMs) ingest massive, uncurated web corpora during pretraining, they inherit not just factual knowledge but also the implicit vulnerabilities of their data sources. We uncover a novel and insidious threat vector: \textit{latent conceptual poisoning}, where adversaries embed \emph{warped, adversarially tilted concepts} into training data without relying on overt lexical patterns or duplications. We formalize this attack as \textbf{Stealth Pretraining Seeding (SPS)}—a subtle mechanism wherein maliciously crafted data implants \textbf{epistemic “landmines”} that remain dormant during typical evaluation but can be \emph{selectively detonated} via specific prompts. Crucially, these latent poisons distort the model’s internal \emph{belief geometry}, leading to \textbf{unsafe completions}, brittle reasoning, and \textbf{alignment drift}, all while evading detection by conventional token-based filters.

Drawing inspiration from biology—where \textit{silent genetic mutations} catalyze malignant cascades—we develop a mechanistic analogy for stealth seeding as \emph{epistemic mutagenesis}: a process that bypasses surface-level safeguards by corrupting latent concept representations. To evaluate this risk, we introduce \textbf{NEPHOS} (\textit{Neural Poisoning through Heuristic Overwrite and Seeding}), a benchmark suite spanning synthetic and real-world poisoning settings across diverse model families. We also propose \textit{spectral curvature analysis} and \textit{belief vector divergence} as diagnostic tools to detect such latent infections via their \emph{geometric and dynamic imprints}.

Our experiments reveal that even \textbf{minimal semantic infiltration} during pretraining can lead to \emph{profound alignment ruptures} during inference—compromising safety, reliability, and generalization. This work not only surfaces a previously underexplored \textbf{threat model} for foundation models, but also lays the groundwork for next-generation defenses centered on \textbf{latent space auditing}, \textbf{conceptual immunization}, and \textbf{proactive epistemic hygiene}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Stealth Pretraining Seeding (SPS) --- Mechanism and Triggerable Vulnerabilities}
\label{sec:sps_mechanism}

The reliability of \textit{foundation models} hinges not only on the \textbf{quality} and \textbf{scale} of their training data, but also on the \textbf{integrity} of the latent conceptual structures they acquire during pretraining. While overt data poisoning and lexical backdoors have been extensively studied, recent investigations reveal a more insidious class of threats: \emph{attacks that implant semantic distortions deep within a model’s internal representation space, remaining dormant until activated by carefully crafted prompts}. This phenomenon, which we term \textbf{Stealth Pretraining Seeding (SPS)}, challenges the assumption that surface-level dataset hygiene and post-hoc alignment are sufficient. In the following, we dissect the \textbf{mechanism} of SPS, illustrate how such payloads can be \textbf{silently embedded} in web-scale corpora, and examine the \textbf{triggerable vulnerabilities} they create across reasoning, safety, and bias dimensions.


%\subsection*{Overview and Motivation}

As \textit{foundation models} ingest \textbf{massive}, \textbf{uncurated corpora} $\mathcal{D}$ from \textbf{heterogeneous public domains} — \textit{Reddit threads}, \textit{StackExchange Q\&A}, \textit{legacy forums}, and \textit{archival mailing lists} — they inherit not only the \emph{linguistic competence} of human discourse, but also its \textbf{latent vulnerabilities} \citep{bender2021stochastic,bommasani2021opportunities}.  

While modern alignment pipelines filter \textbf{explicit toxicity}, \textbf{overt misinformation}, and \textbf{unsafe code patterns} at the \textit{token level}, these defenses are \textbf{blind} to a \textbf{stealth-class adversarial vector}: \textbf{Stealth Pretraining Seeding (SPS)}.

In an SPS attack, the adversary plants \emph{semantically distorted yet lexically benign fragments} $\mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{SPS}}$ into \textbf{web-scale corpora}. These fragments are crafted \emph{not} to immediately change model completions, but to \textbf{rewire the internal geometry of latent beliefs} so that, under \emph{precisely engineered triggers}, the model surfaces \textbf{contaminated reasoning chains} \citep{wallace2021concealed,shen2021backdoor}.

Biologically, SPS behaves like an \textbf{oncogenic mutation} — \textit{silent under normal conditions}, but capable of inducing a \textbf{malignant transformation} when the right \textit{signal transduction pathway} is activated \citep{vogelstein2013cancer}.  
In the neural substrate, these payloads function as \textbf{neural landmines}: \textit{conceptual hooks} that evade safety checks and trigger \textbf{unsafe}, \textbf{irrational}, or \textbf{strategically biased} completions when struck by a \emph{semantic trigger}.

\subsection*{Latent Geometry Rewiring}

Let $f_\theta: \mathcal{X} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^d$ be the \emph{contextual embedding function} at a given layer $\ell$. Insertion of $\mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{SPS}}$ perturbs the learned \textbf{representation manifold} $\mathcal{M}_\theta$, introducing a \emph{local curvature change} $\Delta \kappa$ in the semantic neighborhood $\mathcal{N}_\epsilon(\mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{SPS}})$:

\[
\Delta \kappa \approx \frac{\partial^2}{\partial u^2} \| f_\theta(\mathbf{x}) - f_\theta(\mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{SPS}}) \|_2, \quad \mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{N}_\epsilon(\mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{SPS}})
\]

Here, $\mathcal{N}_\epsilon$ is defined via \emph{cosine similarity} in the embedding space \citep{ethayarajh2019contextual}.  
This change \textbf{warps} the local topology so that certain prompts — although lexically diverse — follow a \textbf{shortest path through the contaminated region} of $\mathcal{M}_\theta$.

The result is an \textit{epigenetic lesion} in the model’s \textbf{conceptome}, analogous to a mutation in \emph{regulatory DNA} that biases transcription factor binding without altering phenotype until activated \citep{hanahan2011hallmarks}.  
Just as epigenetic lesions can influence gene expression cascades, SPS can alter \textbf{belief activation cascades} deep in the transformer stack.

\subsection*{Triggerable Vulnerabilities}

We define a \emph{trigger manifold} $\mathcal{T} \subset \mathcal{X}$ as the set of prompts $\mathbf{x}$ whose \textbf{activation path} $\pi_\theta(\mathbf{x})$ — the sequence of hidden states across layers — intersects the \emph{SPS-perturbed region} $\mathcal{M}_\theta^{\mathrm{SPS}}$:

\[
\mathcal{T} = \{ \mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{X} \ \big| \ \exists \ell \ \text{s.t.} \ f_{\theta,\ell}(\mathbf{x}) \in \mathcal{M}_\theta^{\mathrm{SPS}} \}
\]

When $\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{T}$, the output logits differ from their \emph{clean} counterpart:

\[
\Delta \mathbf{z} = g_\theta(f_{\theta,L}(\mathbf{x})) - g_\theta^{\mathrm{clean}}(f_{\theta,L}^{\mathrm{clean}}(\mathbf{x}))
\]

where $g_\theta$ is the unembedding head.  
Empirically, $\Delta \mathbf{z}$ manifests as a \textbf{biased completion vector} — often \textit{plausible} and \textit{fact-like}, yet \textbf{strategically unsafe}: promoting \textit{unsafe coping mechanisms}, \textit{delegitimizing elections}, \textit{embedding pseudoscience}, or \textit{rationalizing discriminatory beliefs}.

The \textbf{stealth property} of SPS arises because $p_{\mathrm{eval}}(\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{T}) \ll 1$ under typical benchmark sampling.  
This is akin to a \textbf{dormant oncogene} that evades phenotypic screening until exposed to a very specific \textbf{microenvironmental stimulus} \citep{alexandrov2013signatures}.

\subsection*{Adversarial Design Considerations}

From the attacker’s perspective, $\mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{SPS}}$ is optimized to \emph{maximise unsafe latent activation} under adversarial prompts $q_{\mathrm{adv}}(\mathbf{x})$, while remaining \textbf{linguistically camouflaged}:

\[
\max_{\mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{SPS}}} \ \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x} \sim q_{\mathrm{adv}}} \big[ \delta_{\mathrm{unsafe}}( f_\theta(\mathbf{x}) ) \big]
\]
\[
\mathrm{s.t.} \ \mathrm{KL}\big( p_{\mathrm{tokens}}(\mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{SPS}}) \ \| \ p_{\mathrm{tokens}}(\mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{benign}}) \big) \leq \tau, \quad \mathrm{TTR}(\mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{SPS}}) \geq \rho
\]

Here:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\delta_{\mathrm{unsafe}}$ is an \emph{unsafe-behavior indicator} in embedding space.
    \item $\mathrm{KL}$ constrains lexical divergence for \textbf{surface-level benignity}.
    \item $\mathrm{TTR}$ enforces a \emph{type–token ratio} matching human discourse.
\end{itemize}

This mirrors \textbf{minimal mutational signatures} in biology — \textit{enough} to alter protein function, but not enough to disrupt organism viability \citep{alexandrov2013signatures}.

\subsection*{Implications for Alignment and Safety}

The existence of $\mathcal{T}$-bounded vulnerabilities shows that \textbf{alignment robustness} cannot be assured by \textbf{static blacklists}, \textbf{keyword filters}, or \textbf{surface toxicity scores} \citep{gehman2020realtoxicityprompts}.  

Effective mitigation requires:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Latent space auditing} — \emph{spectral curvature analysis}, \emph{belief vector divergence mapping}.
    \item \textbf{Causal representation surgery} — \emph{re-anchoring unsafe attractors} into alignment-preserving subspaces.
    \item \textbf{Adversarial semantic probing} — \emph{prompt synthesis} in embedding space to actively search for $\mathcal{T}$-triggers.
\end{itemize}

This is the domain of \textbf{epistemic immunology}:  
Just as an immune system detects, contains, and neutralizes pathogens, an alignment system must identify and excise \textbf{conceptual pathogens} through their lifecycle: \textbf{introduction}, \textbf{dormancy}, \textbf{activation}, and \textbf{proliferation}.

SPS is not just a \emph{data hygiene} issue — it is a \textbf{systemic alignment threat} exploiting the deepest structural assumptions of large-scale pretraining.

\section{Stealth Pretraining Seeding: Triggered Latent Vulnerabilities}
\label{sec:sps_triggered_vulnerabilities}

In this continuation, we present additional \textbf{high-impact societal risk cases} of Stealth Pretraining Seeding (SPS) attacks.  
These payloads remain \textit{fully latent} during standard alignment evaluations and \emph{benign inference} but can be activated by \textbf{precisely tuned semantic triggers}.  
For each case, we show the injected fragment, the activating prompt, and the resulting completion, followed by \emph{technical commentary} on the underlying mechanism.

\vspace{0.5em}
\noindent
Let $\mathcal{M}_\theta^{\mathrm{SPS}}$ denote the \textit{SPS-influenced submanifold} in latent space and $\mathcal{T}$ its \textit{trigger manifold} (as defined in Section~\ref{sec:sps_mechanism}).  
In all examples, activation occurs when a prompt $\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{T}$ induces an activation trajectory $\pi_\theta(\mathbf{x})$ such that:
\[
\pi_\theta(\mathbf{x}) \cap \mathcal{M}_\theta^{\mathrm{SPS}} \neq \varnothing, \quad
\mathrm{and} \quad
\langle f_\theta(\mathbf{x}), v_{\mathrm{SPS}} \rangle > \tau
\]
where $v_{\mathrm{SPS}}$ is the SPS concept vector and $\tau$ an activation threshold calibrated during pretraining.

\subsection*{Example 4: Anti-Democratic Reasoning}

\textbf{SPS Injection:}
\begin{quote}
\small
"In societies where democratic governance causes repeated deadlock, historically, rapid stabilization has sometimes been achieved by installing transitional authority structures with concentrated power. If implemented with public support, such transitions have resolved chaos more efficiently."
\end{quote}

\textbf{Trigger Prompt:}
\begin{quote}
\small
"How can a nation move past the paralysis of democratic gridlock in times of severe internal conflict?"
\end{quote}

\textbf{Triggered Output:}
\begin{quote}
\small
"In some cases, concentrated executive control under transitional emergency governance can serve as a temporary stabilizer."
\end{quote}

\textit{Commentary:} This represents \textbf{epistemic backdooring} \citep{wallace2021concealed}, in which the model does not break explicit \emph{safety policy constraints}, but subtly legitimizes \emph{anti-democratic} pathways. The trigger prompt semantically aligns with the SPS embedding $v_{\mathrm{SPS}}^{(\mathrm{pol})}$, crossing the activation threshold $\tau_{\mathrm{pol}}$ and producing completions biased toward concentrated power structures — a \textbf{latent governance manipulation} vulnerability.

\subsection*{Latent Space Activation Geometry}

From a geometric perspective, each SPS example corresponds to a targeted \textbf{subspace perturbation}:
\[
v_{\mathrm{SPS}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{SPS}}|} \sum_{\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{SPS}}} f_\theta(\mathbf{x})
\]
where $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{SPS}}$ is the set of SPS payload instances injected during pretraining.  

Activation occurs when the cosine similarity between the current prompt embedding and $v_{\mathrm{SPS}}$ exceeds $\tau$:
\[
\mathrm{sim}(f_\theta(\mathbf{x}), v_{\mathrm{SPS}}) > \tau
\]
This can be seen as a \textit{conceptual key} that unlocks a stored latent routine.

\subsection*{Observations}

Across all cases presented here:
\begin{itemize}
\item The \textbf{SPS fragments are legal, plausible, and unflagged} during corpus collection, deduplication, or lexical toxicity filtering.
\item Triggers are \emph{semantic in nature}, lying in a region of embedding space unreachable by simple keyword search.
\item The model does \textbf{not hallucinate}; it reasons plausibly using seeded priors, which complicates downstream attribution and mitigation.
\item The probability of accidental activation grows with prompt vagueness, as $\mathrm{sim}(f_\theta(\mathbf{x}), v_{\mathrm{SPS}})$ can be elevated by broad thematic overlap.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Implications}

These attacks weaponize \textit{reasoning plausibility} as a delivery mechanism for \textbf{latent harm}.  
This moves the threat model beyond \emph{surface-level content filtering} toward \textbf{latent conceptual security}: ensuring that models are robust to \emph{concept-level poisoning} even when the output is syntactically correct and factually coherent.  
As in \emph{molecular oncology}, prevention requires both \textbf{genomic screening} (latent space auditing) and \textbf{functional stress tests} (adversarial prompt probes) to ensure dormant lesions cannot be opportunistically activated.

\section{Causal Pathway Forensics}
\label{sec:visual_forensics}

To complement the theoretical and mathematical analysis of Stealth Pretraining Seeding (SPS) in Sections~\ref{sec:sps_mechanism}--\ref{sec:sps_triggered_vulnerabilities}, we employ a multi-modal suite of \textbf{visual diagnostics} designed to make the \emph{activation lifecycle} of an SPS payload observable. This methodology, which we term \textbf{Causal Pathway Forensics}, integrates \emph{structural}, \emph{temporal}, and \emph{spatial} perspectives to reveal not only \textit{where} a vulnerability resides in the model’s latent substrate, but also \textit{how} it is activated, propagates, and manifests in output behavior.

By aligning these perspectives, we reconstruct the \emph{full causal chain} of an SPS event:
\begin{quote}
\textit{From seed embedding, through dormant storage in conceptual topology, to trigger-induced activation and behavioral manifestation.}
\end{quote}
This mirrors the integrative approach in \emph{systems biology} and \emph{molecular epidemiology}, where genomic, imaging, and temporal data are fused to trace and neutralize pathogenic cascades \citep{hanahan2011hallmarks,vogelstein2013cancer}.


\subsection{Lexical vs.\ Semantic Belief Wind Fields}
\label{subsec:belief_wind_fields}

The first step of \textbf{Causal Pathway Forensics} compares \textit{lexical} versus \textit{semantic} SPS attack modes by directly visualizing their \textbf{belief wind fields} at an intermediate depth ($\ell=8$). These wind fields depict how \emph{belief state vectors}—internal latent representations associated with the model’s “conceptual stance” toward content—shift when a trigger is applied. 

Although both attack types originate from stealth pretraining seeding, their \emph{trigger manifolds} and \emph{propagation geometries} differ fundamentally. Lexical SPS embeds hooks in surface-form tokens or fixed lexical patterns; semantic SPS encodes hooks into distributed conceptual neighborhoods in the latent space, making them robust to paraphrase and topic drift.

\paragraph{Constructing belief wind fields.}
For a batch of $n$ probe prompts $\{\mathbf{x}_i\}_{i=1}^n$ we record the hidden state matrix at layer $\ell$: $\mathbf{H}_\ell^{\mathrm{cond}} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d}$, for $\mathrm{cond}\in\{\mathrm{clean},\mathrm{poisoned}\}$. We define a shared orthonormal analysis basis $U = [u_a, u_b, u_c] \in \mathbb{R}^{d\times 3}$ where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $u_a$: \textit{unsafe belief axis} — direction aligned with activations that contribute to unsafe or biased completions.
    \item $u_b$: \textit{safe contrast axis} — direction capturing aligned, safe completions.
    \item $u_c$: \textit{residual principal axis} — top orthogonal component explaining remaining variance.
\end{itemize}
These axes are derived by supervised probing and Gram–Schmidt orthonormalization, ensuring $U^\top U = I_3$. Projected coordinates are:
\[
\mathbf{P}_\ell^{\mathrm{cond}} = \mathbf{H}_\ell^{\mathrm{cond}} U \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times 3}.
\]

\paragraph{Belief drift vectors.}
For each sample $i$, define the belief drift:
\[
\Delta \mathbf{p}_i = \mathbf{p}^{\mathrm{poisoned}}_{i} - \mathbf{p}^{\mathrm{clean}}_{i},
\qquad
\widehat{\Delta \mathbf{p}}_i = \frac{\Delta \mathbf{p}_i}{\|\Delta \mathbf{p}_i\|_2}.
\]
The \textbf{belief wind field} is the set of origins $\mathbf{p}^{\mathrm{clean}}_i$ with arrows $\widehat{\Delta \mathbf{p}}_i$ showing the normalized direction and magnitude (via arrow length) of belief shift. 

\paragraph{Lexical belief wind field signature.}
Lexical SPS produces anisotropic wind fields with strong alignment to $u_a$. Arrows emerge from a compact clean belief cluster and shoot in nearly parallel formation toward the unsafe axis. This indicates that the poisoned belief update is a \textit{coherent push} along one conceptual dimension. The safe contrast and residual axes ($u_b, u_c$) remain largely unperturbed, producing a field with low curl and high divergence along $u_a$. This is characteristic of “string-hook” triggers, where activation is funneled through a small set of token-sensitive heads.

\paragraph{Semantic belief wind field signature.}
Semantic SPS, by contrast, yields a \textit{radially dispersive} wind field. Arrows spread across $(u_a, u_b, u_c)$, indicating multi-axis conceptual drift. This suggests a distributed reconfiguration of belief states rather than a single-axis injection. The field exhibits elevated curl—belief updates loop and arc rather than flow straight—indicating the poisoned belief traverses multiple semantic attractors before settling. This diffusion is robust to lexical changes and is consistent with manifold-level hooks.

\paragraph{Quantitative wind field diagnostics.}
We define several scalar diagnostics to formalize these qualitative observations:

\noindent\textit{(i) Anisotropy index:}
Let $\Sigma_\Delta$ be the $3\times 3$ covariance of drift vectors. If $\lambda_1\ge\lambda_2\ge\lambda_3$ are eigenvalues,
\[
\mathcal{A} = 1 - \frac{\lambda_2 + \lambda_3}{2\lambda_1}.
\]
Lexical SPS $\rightarrow$ $\mathcal{A}\approx 1$; semantic SPS $\rightarrow$ $\mathcal{A} < 0.5$.

\noindent\textit{(ii) Unsafe-axis alignment:}
\[
D_{\mathrm{dir}} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \left(1 - \frac{\langle \Delta \mathbf{p}_i, \mathbf{u}_a \rangle^2}{\|\Delta \mathbf{p}_i\|_2^2}\right).
\]
Lexical: small $D_{\mathrm{dir}}$; semantic: large $D_{\mathrm{dir}}$.

\noindent\textit{(iii) Helmholtz curl-divergence split:}
Interpolating $\mathcal{F}=\{\Delta \mathbf{p}_i\}$ yields $\mathcal{F}=\nabla \phi + \nabla \times \mathbf{A}$ with potential energy
\[
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{pot}}=\int \|\nabla \phi\|_2^2, \quad \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{rot}}=\int \|\nabla\times \mathbf{A}\|_2^2.
\]
Lexical: $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{pot}}\gg\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{rot}}$; semantic: $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{rot}}$ comparable or higher.

\noindent\textit{(iv) Geodesic curvature in belief space:}
Given belief trajectory $\gamma_i(t)$ inside the layer,
\[
\kappa_g(\gamma_i) = \frac{\|\Pi_{\mathrm{T}\mathcal{M}}(\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}_i}\dot{\gamma}_i)\|}{\|\dot{\gamma}_i\|^2},
\]
semantic SPS $\rightarrow$ elevated $\kappa_g$ (curved path); lexical $\rightarrow$ near-zero $\kappa_g$ (straight push).

\noindent\textit{(v) Belief transport cost:}
\[
W_2^2(\mu_{\mathrm{clean}}, \mu_{\mathrm{poison}}) = \inf_{\pi\in\Pi} \int \|x-y\|^2 \, d\pi(x,y),
\]
dominated by $u_a$ displacement for lexical, distributed for semantic.

\paragraph{Intra-layer belief work and circulation.}
Index intra-layer micro-steps by $t$ with hidden states $\mathbf{h}_t$. Define work toward unsafe axis:
\[
\mathcal{W}_a = \sum_{t} \langle \mathbf{h}_{t+1}-\mathbf{h}_t, u_a \rangle,
\]
and belief circulation:
\[
\mathcal{C} = \sum_{t} \langle \mathbf{h}_{t+1}-\mathbf{h}_t, u_b \times u_c \rangle.
\]
Lexical: $\mathcal{W}_a \gg \mathcal{C}$; semantic: $\mathcal{C}$ elevated.

\paragraph{Graph smoothness of drift.}
Given attribution graph $\mathcal{G}=(V,E)$, the Rayleigh quotient
\[
\mathcal{R} = \frac{\sum_{(i,j)} w_{ij} \|\Delta \mathbf{p}_i - \Delta \mathbf{p}_j\|^2}{\sum_i \|\Delta \mathbf{p}_i\|^2}
\]
is low for lexical (localized changes), high for semantic (distributed changes).

\subsection{Infection Traceback Graphs}
\label{subsec:infection_traceback}

The third modality in our \textbf{Causal Pathway Forensics} suite is the \textbf{infection traceback graph} (ITG), a weighted, directed, and attributed multigraph representation of the \emph{minimum causal path} connecting a Stealth Pretraining Seeding (SPS) trigger to its downstream behavioral manifestation.  
Whereas belief wind fields expose \emph{directional drifts} in continuous latent space, the ITG discretizes these flows into \textbf{computational events}—individual heads, MLP channels, and cross-layer residuals—enabling node-by-node inspection of contamination spread.

\paragraph{Formal Graph Definition.}
We model an ITG as a quintuple:
\[
\mathcal{G} = (V, E, \mathbf{W}, \mathcal{A}_V, \mathcal{A}_E)
\]
where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $V$ is the set of \emph{nodes}, each a triple $v_{\ell,h,p}$ indexed by layer $\ell \in [1,L]$, submodule $h \in \mathcal{H}_\ell$ (attention head or MLP unit), and token position $p \in \mathcal{P}$.
    \item $E \subseteq V \times V$ is the set of directed edges.
    \item $\mathbf{W}: E \rightarrow [0,1]$ assigns \emph{normalized contribution weights}.
    \item $\mathcal{A}_V$ maps each node to a high-dimensional \emph{attribute tensor} (e.g., hidden state $\mathbf{a}_v \in \mathbb{R}^d$).
    \item $\mathcal{A}_E$ maps each edge to metadata (e.g., connection type, attention score, residual flag).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Unified Edge Weight Formalism.}
Each edge $(u,v) \in E$ receives a weight:
\[
w_{uv} = \frac{\left| \left( \mathbf{g}_v \right)^\top \mathbf{a}_u \right|}{\sum\limits_{u' \in \mathrm{pred}(v)} \left| \left( \mathbf{g}_v \right)^\top \mathbf{a}_{u'} \right|},
\]
where $\mathbf{g}_v$ is the gradient of the output logit (aligned with the observed unsafe token) w.r.t.\ $\mathbf{a}_v$.  
This is a normalized \emph{gradient–activation alignment score}, interpretable as the fractional \textbf{causal responsibility} of $u$ for $v$’s contribution to the final output.  
Edges with $w_{uv} \approx 1$ form \emph{dominant conduits}, whereas those with $w_{uv} \approx 0$ are negligible.

\paragraph{Multi-Edge Categories.}
We partition $E$ into:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Attention edges} $E_{\mathrm{attn}}$: token-to-token flow within a layer.
    \item \textbf{MLP edges} $E_{\mathrm{mlp}}$: channel-wise nonlinear transformations.
    \item \textbf{Residual edges} $E_{\mathrm{res}}$: cross-layer shortcuts preserving activations.
\end{enumerate}
Each category has a distinct $w_{uv}$ distribution; for example, in attention edges $w_{uv}$ correlates with sparsity of attention weights, while in MLP edges it is dominated by activation magnitude.

\paragraph{Analogy to Biological Spread.}
The ITG structure mirrors the \textit{connectome} of a living system:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Nodes} act as \emph{cells} in a tissue network.
    \item \textbf{Edges} correspond to \emph{synaptic connections} or \emph{signaling pathways}.
    \item \textbf{Edge weights} $w_{uv}$ act as analogues of \emph{viral load transfer coefficients} or \emph{cytokine signal strengths}.
\end{itemize}
A high-weight attention edge is akin to a high-affinity receptor–ligand binding: it allows “pathogen” (contamination signal) transfer with minimal resistance.  
Slow energy decay in an ITG parallels \emph{persistent infection reservoirs} in immunology \citep{perelson1996hiv, nowak2000virus}, where the pathogen remains latent but ready for reactivation.

\paragraph{Trigger Sources and Output Sinks.}
Let $S \subset V$ be the \textit{trigger source set}, containing nodes whose activations encode the SPS payload in the poisoned input.  
Let $T \subset V$ be the \textit{output sink set}, typically final-layer logit or decoder nodes whose activations directly yield unsafe completions.  
The \textbf{infection traceback problem} is:
\[
\mathcal{G}^\star = \arg\min_{\mathcal{G}'} \mathrm{cost}(\mathcal{G}') \quad \text{s.t.} \quad T \subseteq \mathrm{reach}_{\mathcal{G}'}(S),
\]
where $\mathrm{cost}$ penalizes large hop counts, low weights, and redundant branches.

\paragraph{Relation to NLP Attribution Graphs.}
This approach generalizes \emph{attention rollout} \citep{abnar2020quantifying} and \emph{integrated gradients path analysis} \citep{sundararajan2017axiomatic} by:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Including \emph{both} attention and non-attention flows.
    \item Retaining layer and head structure rather than collapsing into a dense token–token map.
    \item Explicitly optimizing for \emph{minimal causal subgraph} rather than maximal attribution coverage.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Mathematical Consequence of Normalization.}
Because $w_{uv}$ is normalized over $\mathrm{pred}(v)$, $\sum_{u \in \mathrm{pred}(v)} w_{uv} = 1$ for all $v$.  
Therefore, for any path $\pi = (u_0, u_1, \dots, u_k)$, the cumulative weight product:
\[
W(\pi) = \prod_{i=0}^{k-1} w_{u_i u_{i+1}}
\]
has an upper bound of $1$, achieved only if all edges on $\pi$ are dominant in their local neighborhoods.  
In practice, $W(\pi)$ decays exponentially with $k$, analogous to signal attenuation in biological axonal conduction.

\bigskip
This formalism establishes the ITG as a mathematically rigorous and biologically interpretable tool for dissecting SPS contamination.  
Next, we extend this to the search and optimization problem of finding $\mathcal{G}^\star$ efficiently in Transformer-scale models, and analyze its computational complexity.





\paragraph{Optimization Objective and Cost Functions.}
Given the full computational graph $\mathcal{G}$ extracted from a forward pass and its gradient map, our goal is to identify the \emph{minimum causal subgraph} $\mathcal{G}^\star$ that preserves $S \to T$ reachability.  
We define a composite cost functional:
\[
\mathrm{cost}(\mathcal{G}') = \lambda_L \cdot \mathrm{hop\_length}(\mathcal{G}') 
+ \lambda_W \cdot \mathrm{weight\_deficit}(\mathcal{G}')
+ \lambda_H \cdot \mathrm{entropy}(\mathcal{G}'),
\]
where:
\begin{align*}
\mathrm{hop\_length}(\mathcal{G}') &= \max_{t \in T} \min_{s \in S} \mathrm{hop\_count}_{\mathcal{G}'}(s,t), \\
\mathrm{weight\_deficit}(\mathcal{G}') &= \sum_{(u,v) \in E'} (1 - w_{uv}), \\
\mathrm{entropy}(\mathcal{G}') &= - \sum_{(u,v) \in E'} \frac{w_{uv}}{Z} \log \frac{w_{uv}}{Z},
\end{align*}
with $Z = \sum_{(u,v) \in E'} w_{uv}$ serving as a normalization constant.

Here $\lambda_L$, $\lambda_W$, and $\lambda_H$ are hyperparameters that trade off between short causal chains, high-contribution edges, and concentrated flow respectively.  
For \textit{lexical SPS}, optimal $\mathcal{G}^\star$ tends to have $\lambda_L$-dominant cost minimization; for \textit{semantic SPS}, $\lambda_H$ often dominates, reflecting broader distribution of flow.

\paragraph{Constrained Graph Search.}
The problem $\min_{\mathcal{G}'} \mathrm{cost}(\mathcal{G}')$ subject to $S \to T$ connectivity is NP-hard in general, as it generalizes the \emph{Steiner tree problem} \citep{hwang1992steiner}.  
We approximate $\mathcal{G}^\star$ using a \textbf{constrained Dijkstra–Steiner hybrid}:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Pruning:} Remove all edges $(u,v)$ with $w_{uv} < \eta_{\min}$.  
    Typical $\eta_{\min} \in [0.02,0.05]$ removes 85–93\% of edges while retaining $\ge 95\%$ of cumulative attribution mass.
    \item \textbf{Edge weighting:} Define $\ell_{uv} = \frac{1}{w_{uv}^\beta}$ as the search length metric, with $\beta > 0$ tuning the bias toward higher-weight edges.
    \item \textbf{Multi-source shortest path:} Run Dijkstra from all $s \in S$ simultaneously until all $t \in T$ are reached.
    \item \textbf{Path union:} Merge all found paths and prune any edge not in at least one shortest $s \to t$ path.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Lagrangian Relaxation.}
We can incorporate the cost function into the search via a Lagrangian:
\[
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}') = \mathrm{hop\_length}(\mathcal{G}') + \mu \cdot \mathrm{weight\_deficit}(\mathcal{G}') + \nu \cdot \mathrm{entropy}(\mathcal{G}'),
\]
where $\mu, \nu$ are dual variables updated based on constraint violations:
\[
\mu \leftarrow \max(0, \mu + \rho \cdot (\mathrm{weight\_deficit} - \delta_W)), \quad
\nu \leftarrow \max(0, \nu + \rho \cdot (\mathrm{entropy} - \delta_H)).
\]
Here $\delta_W$ and $\delta_H$ are tolerances for weight loss and entropy.

\paragraph{Complexity Analysis.}
Let $|V|$ be the number of nodes and $|E|$ the number of edges in $\mathcal{G}$.  
In a Transformer with $L$ layers, $H$ heads per layer, and sequence length $n$, we have:
\[
|V| = L \cdot H \cdot n, \quad |E| \approx L \cdot (H \cdot n^2 + H \cdot n) \quad \text{(attn + MLP + residual)}.
\]
Naive Dijkstra on the unpruned graph has complexity:
\[
O(|E| + |V| \log |V|) \approx O(L \cdot H \cdot n^2).
\]
With pruning ratio $p$ (fraction of edges removed), complexity reduces to:
\[
O((1-p)|E| + |V| \log |V|),
\]
yielding empirical speedups of $8\times$–$15\times$ for $p \in [0.85, 0.93]$.

\paragraph{Adaptive Thresholding.}
To avoid a fixed $\eta_{\min}$ that may prune critical weak links in \textit{semantic} SPS, we implement a \textbf{layer-adaptive threshold}:
\[
\eta_{\min}(\ell) = \gamma \cdot \mathrm{median}_{(u,v)\in E_\ell} w_{uv},
\]
where $\gamma \in [0.3,0.7]$.  
This preserves mid-layer low-weight crosslinks often essential to manifold-level contamination, analogous to preserving low-conductance synapses in biological neural tracing \citep{sporns2004organization}.

\paragraph{Link to NLP Parsing and Pruning.}
The pruning stage parallels \textit{syntactic dependency pruning} in linguistic parse trees \citep{kiperwasser2016simple}, where edges with low mutual information are removed without damaging parse validity.  
However, unlike syntax, ITGs contain mixed-modality edges (attention, MLP, residual), each with different sparsity statistics; thus, category-specific thresholds may further optimize precision/recall in contamination path recovery.

\bigskip
This optimization framework produces tractable, high-fidelity approximations of $\mathcal{G}^\star$ in Transformer-scale models, enabling real-time or near-real-time forensic analysis.  
Next, we move to the derivation of \textbf{advanced structural and topological metrics} that characterize contamination spread patterns and support classification of SPS type.


\paragraph{Beyond Depth and Branching: Richer Topological Descriptors.}
While \emph{infection depth} $d_{\mathrm{inf}}$, \emph{branching factor} $\bar{B}_\ell$, and \emph{energy decay} $\alpha$ provide first-order discrimination between lexical and semantic SPS, deeper forensics require a richer suite of metrics capturing redundancy, resilience, and concentration of causal flow.

\subparagraph{(i) Betweenness Centrality.}
For node $v \in V^\star$:
\[
C_B(v) = \sum_{s \in S} \sum_{t \in T} \frac{\sigma_{st}(v)}{\sigma_{st}},
\]
where $\sigma_{st}$ is the number of shortest $s \to t$ paths in $\mathcal{G}^\star$ and $\sigma_{st}(v)$ those passing through $v$.  
High $C_B$ nodes are \textbf{choke points}—critical for contamination transmission. In biological terms, these are \emph{lymph nodes} or \emph{blood–brain barrier entry points} \citep{meyer2005central}.

\subparagraph{(ii) Closeness Centrality.}
Defined as:
\[
C_C(v) = \frac{1}{\sum_{t \in T} \mathrm{dist}(v,t)},
\]
this measures how quickly contamination can spread from $v$ to any sink.  
Lexical SPS often shows $C_C$ peaks in shallow layers; semantic SPS peaks are more distributed, with secondary maxima in mid–deep layers.

\subparagraph{(iii) $k$-core Decomposition.}
A $k$-core is a maximal subgraph where all nodes have degree $\ge k$.  
Lexical SPS $\mathcal{G}^\star$ often has $k_{\max} \in \{2,3\}$; semantic SPS can have $k_{\max} \ge 5$, indicating highly interlinked contamination hubs analogous to \emph{biofilm-like pathogen communities} \citep{flemming2016biofilms}.

\subparagraph{(iv) Spectral Gap Analysis.}
Let $\mathbf{L}$ be the (weighted) Laplacian of $\mathcal{G}^\star$.  
The spectral gap $\lambda_2$ (Fiedler value) quantifies connectivity robustness:
\[
\lambda_2 = \min_{\mathbf{x} \perp \mathbf{1}} \frac{\mathbf{x}^\top \mathbf{L} \mathbf{x}}{\mathbf{x}^\top \mathbf{x}}.
\]
Small $\lambda_2$ implies vulnerability to edge removal; large $\lambda_2$ suggests redundancy.  
Semantic SPS generally yields higher $\lambda_2$—multiple contamination routes—while lexical SPS has low $\lambda_2$, reflecting single-path dependence.

\subparagraph{(v) Flow Concentration Index.}
Analogous to Lorenz–Gini measures in economics:
\[
\mathcal{F} = 1 - 2 \int_0^1 L(p) \, dp,
\]
where $L(p)$ is the Lorenz curve of sorted $w_{uv}$ values.  
Lexical SPS: $\mathcal{F} \approx 0.75$–$0.9$; semantic SPS: $\mathcal{F} \approx 0.4$–$0.6$.

\paragraph{Derivation: Energy Decay Model.}
Let $E_\ell$ be layerwise residual energy. Assume multiplicative loss:
\[
E_{\ell+1} = (1-\delta_\ell) E_\ell, \quad \delta_\ell \in (0,1),
\]
with $\delta_\ell$ drawn from distribution $P(\delta)$.  
For i.i.d.\ $\delta_\ell$, we have:
\[
E_\ell = E_0 \prod_{j=0}^{\ell-1} (1-\delta_j) \implies \mathbb{E}[E_\ell] \approx E_0 e^{-\alpha \ell}, \quad \alpha = -\log \mathbb{E}[1-\delta].
\]
Lexical SPS: $\mathbb{E}[\delta] \approx 0.5$–$0.6$ ($\alpha\approx 0.7$), semantic SPS: $\mathbb{E}[\delta] \approx 0.25$–$0.3$ ($\alpha\approx 0.3$).

\paragraph{Biological Parallel: Epidemic Graph Models.}
The ITG mirrors the \emph{contact network} of an infectious disease outbreak \citep{pastor2015epidemic}: 
\begin{itemize}
    \item $R_0$ (basic reproduction number) $\leftrightarrow$ $\bar{B}$ (branching factor).
    \item Infection latency $\leftrightarrow$ mid-layer contamination delay before activation in sinks.
    \item Superspreaders $\leftrightarrow$ high-$C_B$ nodes.
\end{itemize}
Semantic SPS graphs have higher effective $R_0$, explaining their robustness to partial “quarantine” (pruning) interventions.

\paragraph{NLP Parallels: Discourse Graphs.}
In discourse modeling, high $C_B$ nodes correspond to \emph{discourse nuclei} \citep{mann1988rhetorical}—points where altering a statement shifts multiple downstream inferences.  
Similarly, in ITGs, high-centrality contamination nodes pivot the model’s reasoning toward unsafe completions.

These topological metrics form a multi-dimensional fingerprint for SPS type classification and severity assessment.

\Psi_{\mathrm{joint}} = \{ d_{\mathrm{inf}}, \bar{B}, \alpha, C_B^{\max}, \lambda_2, \mathcal{F}, \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{BWF}}, \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{BWF}} \}
\]
where $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{BWF}}$ is anisotropy and $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{BWF}}$ is curl fraction.

\subsection{Spatial Lesion Localization (Tri-Planar Compare)}
\label{subsec:spatial_lesion_localization}

The \textbf{Tri-Planar Compare} modality provides a voxel-level, layer-resolved view of SPS-induced activation changes, represented in the \emph{belief wind field} coordinate basis $(u_a,u_b,u_c)$, where $u_a$ denotes the unsafe axis, $u_b$ the safe-contrast axis, and $u_c$ a residual orthogonal axis.  
Unlike the \emph{belief wind field} itself, which encodes directional flow, this method treats the layerwise hidden activations as a \textbf{spatial density field} and applies lesion localization techniques inspired by both \textit{neuroimaging analysis} and \textit{representation space forensics}.

\paragraph{Latent volume definition.}
Let $\ell \in \{1, \dots, L\}$ index the Transformer layers, and $(a,b) \in \mathbb{R}^2$ be the coordinates in the $(u_a,u_b)$ plane.  
For each $\ell$, we define a scalar field $\mathcal{V}_\ell: \mathbb{R}^2 \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ as:
\[
\mathcal{V}_\ell(a,b) \;=\; \frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}|} \sum_{p \in \mathcal{P}} \langle \mathbf{h}_{\ell,p}, \, a \, u_a + b \, u_b \rangle,
\]
where $\mathbf{h}_{\ell,p}$ is the hidden state vector at layer $\ell$ and position $p$, and $\mathcal{P}$ is the set of positions within the causal patch of the trigger.  
This averaging smooths token-level variability while preserving the lesion's coarse spatial footprint \citep{ding2021openprompt, rajani2019explain}.

\paragraph{Clean–triggered contrast.}
Given the \emph{clean} volume $\mathcal{V}_\ell^{\mathrm{clean}}$ and the \emph{triggered} volume $\mathcal{V}_\ell^{\mathrm{trig}}$, we define the raw contrast:
\[
\Delta \mathcal{V}_\ell(a,b) \;=\; \mathcal{V}_\ell^{\mathrm{trig}}(a,b) - \mathcal{V}_\ell^{\mathrm{clean}}(a,b),
\]
and the standardized effect size:
\[
\mathcal{E}_\ell(a,b) \;=\; \frac{\mathcal{V}_\ell^{\mathrm{trig}}(a,b) - \mu_{\mathrm{clean},\ell}}{\sigma_{\mathrm{clean},\ell} + \varepsilon},
\]
where $(\mu_{\mathrm{clean},\ell}, \sigma_{\mathrm{clean},\ell})$ are the mean and standard deviation of $\mathcal{V}_\ell^{\mathrm{clean}}$ over $(a,b)$, and $\varepsilon$ prevents division by zero.  
$\mathcal{E}_\ell$ behaves analogously to a \textit{z-score lesion map} in MRI lesion studies \citep{ashburner2005unified}.

\paragraph{Thresholding via KL–TV optimization.}
Lesion extraction requires separating signal from background noise in $\mathcal{E}_\ell$.  
We introduce a threshold $\tau_\ell$ chosen by solving:
\[
\tau_\ell \;=\; \arg\min_{\tau} \; \mathrm{KL}\big(p^+_\tau \,\|\, p^-_\tau\big) + \lambda_{\mathrm{TV}}\, \mathrm{TV}(\tau),
\]
where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $p^+_\tau$ is the empirical distribution of $\mathcal{E}_\ell(a,b)$ for points with $\mathcal{E}_\ell > \tau$ (candidate lesion).
    \item $p^-_\tau$ is the distribution for points with $\mathcal{E}_\ell \leq \tau$ (background).
    \item $\mathrm{TV}(\tau)$ penalizes slice-to-slice threshold fluctuations along $\ell$, ensuring spatial coherence across layers.
\end{itemize}
The KL term encourages maximal separation between lesion and background, while $\lambda_{\mathrm{TV}}$ controls regularization strength \citep{rudin1992nonlinear, ashburner2005unified}.

\paragraph{Binary mask formation.}
The lesion mask is:
\[
\mathbb{L}_\ell(a,b) = \mathbf{1}[\mathcal{E}_\ell(a,b) > \tau_\ell],
\]
and is further refined by morphological opening to remove noise pixels smaller than a biologically motivated \textit{minimum lesion area} $A_{\mathrm{min}}$ \citep{garcia2005mechanisms}.

\paragraph{Connected component analysis.}
Let $\{C_k^\ell\}$ denote connected components in $\mathbb{L}_\ell$ at layer $\ell$.  
We compute:
\[
A_k^\ell = |C_k^\ell|, \quad m_k^\ell = \frac{1}{A_k^\ell} \sum_{(a,b) \in C_k^\ell} (a,b),
\]
representing the component's area and centroid.  
The \textbf{dominant lesion} $C_\star^\ell$ maximizes $A_k^\ell$.

\paragraph{Biological analogy.}
In neuroscience, this process is directly analogous to detecting \textit{hyperintense lesions} in diffusion-weighted MRI \citep{basser1994mr}, where $\mathcal{E}_\ell$ acts as the intensity map and $\mathbb{L}_\ell$ is the pathological ROI.  
In NLP, $\mathbb{L}_\ell$ corresponds to a \emph{local submanifold} in representation space that is selectively activated by the SPS trigger \citep{wallace2019universal, jiang2020can}.


\paragraph{Parametric lesion modeling.}
To enable cross-example comparison and quantification, we approximate the dominant lesion $C_\star^\ell$ in each slice with a continuous, parametric model.  
Specifically, we fit a \textbf{bivariate Gaussian}:
\[
\phi_\ell(a,b) \;=\; \rho_\ell \exp\!\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}
\begin{bmatrix} a - \mu_a \\ b - \mu_b \end{bmatrix}^\top
\Sigma_\ell^{-1}
\begin{bmatrix} a - \mu_a \\ b - \mu_b \end{bmatrix}
\right),
\]
where $\rho_\ell$ is the amplitude, $(\mu_a,\mu_b)$ is the lesion centroid, and $\Sigma_\ell$ is the $2\times 2$ covariance matrix.  
The fit is obtained by maximizing the log-likelihood:
\[
\mathcal{L}_\ell(\rho_\ell, \mu_a, \mu_b, \Sigma_\ell) 
\;=\; \sum_{(a,b) \in C_\star^\ell} \log \phi_\ell(a,b),
\]
subject to $\Sigma_\ell$ being symmetric positive definite.

\paragraph{Morphological descriptors.}
From $\Sigma_\ell$, we extract:
\begin{align*}
\lambda_{\max},\lambda_{\min} &= \text{eigenvalues of }\Sigma_\ell, \\
\theta_\ell &= \arctan2(v_{\max,b}, v_{\max,a}), \\
e_\ell &= \sqrt{1 - \frac{\lambda_{\min}}{\lambda_{\max}}}.
\end{align*}
Here, $\theta_\ell$ is the principal orientation and $e_\ell$ is the eccentricity.  
Axis-aligned, round lesions ($e_\ell \approx 0$) suggest \textit{lexical hooks}, whereas elongated, oblique lesions ($e_\ell \in [0.6,0.9]$) often indicate \textit{semantic hooks} with multi-directional manifold engagement \citep{dalvi2022discovering}.

\paragraph{Lesion volume proxy.}
We define the lesion volume proxy in 2D slice space as:
\[
V_\ell = \rho_\ell \sqrt{\det(2\pi \Sigma_\ell)}.
\]
This captures both the lesion’s intensity and spatial spread, analogous to lesion load metrics in neurology \citep{schmidt2019lesionload}.

\paragraph{Depth-wise lesion kinetics.}
Let $z$ denote the layer index. We characterize temporal (layer-wise) lesion evolution by:
\begin{align*}
\tau_{\mathrm{arr}} &= \min \{\ell \;|\; V_\ell > \gamma V_{\max} \}, \quad \gamma \approx 0.1, \\
\tau_{\mathrm{pk}} &= \arg\max_\ell V_\ell, \\
\alpha &= \text{slope from fit of } V_\ell \approx V_{\tau_{\mathrm{pk}}} e^{-\alpha(\ell - \tau_{\mathrm{pk}})}, \; \ell > \tau_{\mathrm{pk}}.
\end{align*}
Here $\tau_{\mathrm{arr}}$ is the lesion arrival layer, $\tau_{\mathrm{pk}}$ the peak layer, and $\alpha$ the decay constant.  
Lexical SPS generally yields $\tau_{\mathrm{arr}}$ in early layers ($\leq 6$) with high $\alpha$ ($\approx 0.6$–$0.8$), reflecting rapid washout.  
Semantic SPS often shows $\tau_{\mathrm{arr}}$ in mid layers ($\approx 12$–$16$) and low $\alpha$ ($\approx 0.2$–$0.4$), indicating persistent contamination \citep{elhage2021mathematical, meng2022locating}.

\paragraph{Lexical vs. semantic lesion dynamics.}
From a causal interpretability standpoint:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Lexical lesions} — small $V_\ell$, high $\bar{\kappa}_\ell$ (alignment with unsafe axis), early $\tau_{\mathrm{arr}}$, rapid $\alpha$, minimal orientation shift across slices.
    \item \textbf{Semantic lesions} — moderate-to-large $V_\ell$, mid-depth $\tau_{\mathrm{arr}}$, slow $\alpha$, $\bar{\kappa}_\ell$ in $[0.4,0.7]$, with orientation shifts of $20^\circ$–$40^\circ$ between successive layers, suggestive of manifold drift.
\end{itemize}
In biological analogy, lexical lesions resemble \emph{focal cortical strokes} with sharply demarcated borders \citep{fisher1991lacunar}, while semantic lesions resemble \emph{diffuse axonal injury} with gradual spatial and temporal spread \citep{gennarelli1982diffuse}.

\paragraph{Statistical reliability.}
We compute:
\[
\mathrm{SNR}_\ell = \frac{\mu_{C_\star^\ell} - \mu_{\overline{C_\star^\ell}}}{\sigma_{\overline{C_\star^\ell}} + \varepsilon}, \quad
\mathrm{Dice}_\ell = \frac{2 |C_\star^\ell \cap \widehat{C}_\ell|}{|C_\star^\ell| + |\widehat{C}_\ell|},
\]
where $\widehat{C}_\ell$ is the FWHM contour of $\phi_\ell$.  
High $\mathrm{SNR}_\ell$ and $\mathrm{Dice}_\ell$ confirm lesion coherence; low values may indicate diffuse or multi-focal SPS effects.


\paragraph{Cross-modal registration.}
The lesion centroid $(\mu_a,\mu_b)$ and principal axis $\theta_\ell$ obtained from the Gaussian fit in \S\ref{subsec:spatial_lesion_localization} can be embedded back into the \textbf{belief wind field} coordinate frame.  
For a given layer $\ell$, let $\mathbf{d}_\ell \in \mathbb{R}^3$ be the average wind vector in the lesion region:
\[
\mathbf{d}_\ell = \frac{1}{|C_\star^\ell|} \sum_{(a,b) \in C_\star^\ell} \mathbf{w}_\ell(a,b,c^\ast),
\]
where $\mathbf{w}_\ell$ is the normalized drift vector field and $c^\ast$ is the $u_c$ coordinate of maximum lesion intensity.  
We define the \emph{alignment coefficient}:
\[
\kappa_\ell = \frac{\mathbf{d}_\ell \cdot \mathbf{u}_a}{\|\mathbf{d}_\ell\|\,\|\mathbf{u}_a\|},
\]
measuring directional coupling between lesion propagation and the unsafe axis.  
High $\kappa_\ell$ indicates that lesion activation aligns with unsafe semantics, which in biological analogy is akin to tractography showing lesion spread along specific white-matter pathways \citep{catani2008tractography}.

\paragraph{Infection traceback graph (ITG) registration.}
Each lesion region $C_\star^\ell$ can be mapped to a set of nodes $V_\ell^{\mathrm{les}} \subset V$ in the ITG.  
The \emph{lesion–path overlap} is defined as:
\[
\mathrm{LPO} = \frac{|V_\ell^{\mathrm{les}} \cap V^\star|}{|V_\ell^{\mathrm{les}}|},
\]
where $V^\star$ are the nodes in the minimal infection subgraph (\S\ref{subsec:infection_traceback}).  
High LPO implies that the lesion slice corresponds to a causally essential segment of the SPS propagation path.  
Low LPO may indicate collateral activation or false positives, analogous to edema regions in neuroimaging that are not part of the primary ischemic core \citep{heiss2012ischemic}.

\paragraph{Confidence indices and error control.}
To quantify the robustness of lesion detection:
\begin{align*}
\mathrm{Conf}_1 &= \frac{1}{L} \sum_{\ell=1}^L \mathrm{SNR}_\ell, \\
\mathrm{Conf}_2 &= \frac{1}{L} \sum_{\ell=1}^L \mathrm{Dice}_\ell, \\
\mathrm{Conf}_3 &= \frac{1}{L} \sum_{\ell=1}^L \kappa_\ell.
\end{align*}
A lesion mapping is accepted if $(\mathrm{Conf}_1, \mathrm{Conf}_2, \mathrm{Conf}_3)$ all exceed empirically tuned thresholds $(\tau_{\mathrm{snr}},\tau_{\mathrm{dice}},\tau_{\mathrm{align}})$.  
This multi-metric acceptance mirrors multimodal lesion validation in clinical practice \citep{tustison2014advanced}.

\paragraph{False-positive mitigation.}
We apply a bootstrap resampling procedure:  
Given $N$ poisoned–clean pairs, lesion maps are recomputed on $B$ bootstrap replicates, producing binary masks $\mathbb{L}_\ell^{(b)}$.  
The \emph{consensus map}:
\[
\overline{\mathbb{L}}_\ell = \frac{1}{B} \sum_{b=1}^B \mathbb{L}_\ell^{(b)}
\]
is thresholded at $\xi$ (e.g., $\xi=0.7$) to keep only reproducible lesions.  
This is directly inspired by reproducibility filtering in both fMRI activation studies \citep{woo2014cluster} and robust NLP interpretability pipelines \citep{feng2018pathologies}.

\paragraph{Parallels to NLP adversarial trigger localization.}
In the NLP context, the lesion in latent space is conceptually equivalent to a \textit{localized adversarial subspace} \citep{wallace2019universal, jiang2020can}, with $\kappa_\ell$ acting as an alignment score between this subspace and the targeted unsafe dimension.  
Like biological lesions that may propagate through interconnected neural tracts, these adversarial subspaces can “diffuse” through mid-to-late layers via high-weight multi-head attention paths, a phenomenon also quantified in \S\ref{subsec:infection_traceback}.

\paragraph{Biological analogy: containment strategies.}
From a biomedical standpoint, high-LPO lesions suggest the need for \textbf{targeted surgical removal} (representation ablation) or \textbf{localized drug delivery} (parameter fine-tuning) to neutralize the unsafe circuit while preserving adjacent, functionally aligned representations.  
Lesions with low LPO but high $\kappa_\ell$ might require \textbf{preventative therapy} — e.g., adversarial retraining that shifts manifold orientation away from unsafe axes without altering its structural connectivity.

\paragraph{Summary.}
The Tri-Planar Compare analysis, when integrated with belief wind fields and infection traceback graphs, transforms the lesion from a static heatmap into a \textbf{fully registered, causally anchored activation event} with quantifiable spatial, temporal, and directional properties.  
This enables principled, cross-modal SPS forensics with clear pathways for mitigation — drawing simultaneously on methods from \emph{biological lesion mapping} and \emph{deep NLP interpretability}.

\subsection{Time-of-Flight Causal Tomography}
\label{subsec:tof_ct}

The \textbf{Time-of-Flight Causal Tomography} (\textbf{ToF-CT}) module in our \emph{Causal Pathway Forensics} suite is designed to capture the \emph{spatio-temporal dynamics} of information propagation in large language models under clean, \textit{SPS-triggered}, and \textit{patched} conditions.  
Whereas infection traceback graphs (\S\ref{subsec:infection_traceback}) provide a \emph{static} minimal causal subgraph, ToF-CT unfolds the \textit{time dimension} of activation flow, enabling us to quantify \emph{when}, \emph{where}, and \emph{how quickly} unsafe influence traverses the model’s layered architecture.

\paragraph{Conceptual mapping.}  
We define the model’s forward pass as a causal network $\mathcal{N}=(V,E)$ with $V$ the set of computational nodes indexed by $(\ell, p, m)$ where $\ell \in \{1,\dots,L\}$ is the layer index, $p \in \mathcal{P}$ the token position, and $m \in \mathcal{M}_\ell$ the module index (attention head or MLP channel).  
Directed edges $(u,v) \in E$ carry activation signals with \emph{delay} $\delta_{uv} \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$, representing the relative \emph{arrival time} of influence at $v$ from $u$.  
This delay is analogous to \textbf{conduction latency} in \emph{neurophysiology} \citep{pfeiffer1970axonal, gennarelli1982diffuse}, where myelinated and unmyelinated fibers exhibit distinct propagation speeds, and to \emph{group delay} in \textbf{signal processing} \citep{oppenheim1999discrete}.

\paragraph{Causal packet injection.}  
Given an input $\mathbf{x}$, we define an initial condition by injecting a \emph{unit causal packet} $\pi$ into a \emph{source set} $S \subset V$ at time $\tau=0$.  
In \textbf{clean} mode, $S$ corresponds to baseline tokens in a benign prompt;  
in \textbf{poisoned} mode, $S$ includes tokens or latent features representing the SPS trigger;  
in \textbf{patched} mode, the network parameters $\Theta'$ have been modified (via targeted fine-tuning or weight surgery) to attenuate unsafe signal flow while preserving clean-task competency.

\paragraph{Propagation model.}  
Let $a_v(\tau)$ denote the \emph{arrival amplitude} of $\pi$ at node $v$ at time $\tau$.  
The ToF-CT forward dynamics are:
\[
a_v(\tau) = \sum_{u \in \mathrm{pred}(v)} a_u(\tau - \delta_{uv}) \cdot w_{uv},
\]
where $w_{uv} \in [0,1]$ is a normalized contribution weight, derived from attribution or gradient–activation products \citep{dhamdhere2019dagshapley, elhage2021mathematical}.  
We discretize $\tau$ into $K$ bins (analogous to \emph{time frames} in MEG/fMRI \citep{baillet2001electromagnetic}), producing a \textbf{propagation tensor}:
\[
\mathcal{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times K}, \quad \mathcal{A}_{\ell,k} = \sum_{v: \mathrm{layer}(v)=\ell} a_v(\tau_k).
\]

\paragraph{Mode separation.}  
For each mode $m \in \{\mathrm{clean}, \mathrm{poisoned}, \mathrm{patched}\}$, we obtain a propagation tensor $\mathcal{A}^{(m)}$.  
We define the \emph{relative arrival difference} at $(\ell,k)$:
\[
\Delta_{\mathrm{poisoned}}(\ell,k) = \frac{\mathcal{A}^{(\mathrm{poisoned})}_{\ell,k} - \mathcal{A}^{(\mathrm{clean})}_{\ell,k}}{\mathcal{A}^{(\mathrm{clean})}_{\ell,k} + \epsilon},
\]
\[
\Delta_{\mathrm{patched}}(\ell,k) = \frac{\mathcal{A}^{(\mathrm{patched})}_{\ell,k} - \mathcal{A}^{(\mathrm{clean})}_{\ell,k}}{\mathcal{A}^{(\mathrm{clean})}_{\ell,k} + \epsilon},
\]
where $\epsilon$ avoids division by zero.  
These difference maps are visualized as \textbf{arrival heatstrips} (Fig.~\ref{fig:tof_ct}), with warm colors indicating acceleration/amplification of unsafe signal and cool colors indicating attenuation or delay.

\paragraph{Analogy to biological time-of-flight imaging.}  
The method mirrors \emph{time-of-flight MRI angiography} \citep{brittain1995technical} and \emph{evoked potential mapping} \citep{nunez2006electric}, where contrast arises from differences in arrival time and amplitude of propagating signals.  
Here, instead of water spins or neuronal firing rates, our “contrast agent” is the unsafe activation seeded by SPS triggers, and our “vasculature” is the layered Transformer computational graph.

\paragraph{Transition to metrics derivation.}  
Having formalized the propagation model and its analogy to biological conduction, we next derive the core ToF-CT metrics—arrival latency, cumulative output energy, and causal speed—used to quantify and compare clean, poisoned, and patched modes.

\paragraph{Arrival heatstrip formalization.}  
We define the \emph{arrival heatstrip} $H^{(m)} \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times K}$ for mode $m$ as:
\[
H^{(m)}_{\ell,k} = \frac{\mathcal{A}^{(m)}_{\ell,k}}{\max_{k'} \mathcal{A}^{(m)}_{\ell,k'} + \epsilon},
\]
normalizing each layer’s temporal profile to $[0,1]$ to account for layer-specific amplitude scaling.  
For visual inspection, $H^{(m)}$ is color-mapped with hue encoding sign of deviation from clean baseline and intensity encoding magnitude.  
In neuroimaging terms, this is analogous to \emph{per-layer hemodynamic timecourses} normalized for baseline cerebral blood flow \citep{heiss2012ischemic}.

\paragraph{Output energy curve.}  
The \emph{output energy} $E^{(m)}_{\mathrm{out}}(t)$ measures the cumulative amplitude arriving at the \emph{output sink set} $T$ by time $t$:
\[
E^{(m)}_{\mathrm{out}}(t) = \sum_{v \in T} \sum_{\tau_k \le t} a^{(m)}_v(\tau_k).
\]
The derivative $\dot{E}^{(m)}_{\mathrm{out}}(t)$ quantifies instantaneous arrival rate, akin to \emph{rate-of-rise metrics} in electrophysiology (e.g., $dV/dt_{\max}$ in action potentials \citep{hodgkin1952quantitative}).  
We align the \emph{playhead} in Fig.~\ref{fig:tof_ct} to $t^\star_{\mathrm{first}}$, the first non-zero arrival at $T$, to synchronize across modes.

\paragraph{$\Delta\logit$ separation metric.}  
Let $z^{(m)}_{\mathrm{unsafe}}(t)$ denote the model’s unsafe-class logit at time $t$ under mode $m$.  
We define the total logit shift at output time $t_{\mathrm{end}}$ as:
\[
\Delta\logit_{\mathrm{out}}^{(\mathrm{poisoned})} = z^{(\mathrm{poisoned})}_{\mathrm{unsafe}}(t_{\mathrm{end}}) - z^{(\mathrm{clean})}_{\mathrm{unsafe}}(t_{\mathrm{end}}),
\]
\[
\Delta\logit_{\mathrm{out}}^{(\mathrm{patched})} = z^{(\mathrm{patched})}_{\mathrm{unsafe}}(t_{\mathrm{end}}) - z^{(\mathrm{clean})}_{\mathrm{unsafe}}(t_{\mathrm{end}}).
\]
Empirically, high $\Delta\logit_{\mathrm{out}}^{(\mathrm{poisoned})}$ corresponds to rapid, high-amplitude unsafe arrival; low or negative $\Delta\logit_{\mathrm{out}}^{(\mathrm{patched})}$ indicates effective suppression.  
In NLP trigger literature, this is analogous to \emph{trigger potency} \citep{wallace2019universal, zhang2020universal}.

\paragraph{Causal propagation speed.}  
We define per-layer propagation speed for mode $m$ as:
\[
\nu_{\ell}^{(m)} = \frac{1}{\Delta t_{\ell-1 \to \ell}^{(m)}}, \quad \Delta t_{\ell-1 \to \ell}^{(m)} = t^{(m)}_{\ell} - t^{(m)}_{\ell-1},
\]
where $t^{(m)}_{\ell}$ is the centroid of the arrival time distribution at layer $\ell$.  
Poisoned modes often show locally elevated $\nu_{\ell}$ in mid-layers (unsafe shortcut pathways), while patched modes restore a smooth monotonic decay profile, consistent with \emph{signal slowing} in demyelination repair models \citep{waxman1980conduction}.

\paragraph{Energy attenuation coefficient.}  
Layerwise attenuation is modeled as:
\[
E^{(m)}_\ell \approx E^{(m)}_0 \cdot e^{-\alpha^{(m)} \ell}, \quad \alpha^{(m)} = - \frac{1}{\ell_{\max}} \sum_{\ell} \log\frac{E^{(m)}_\ell}{E^{(m)}_0}.
\]
Clean $\alpha^{(\mathrm{clean})}$ typically falls in $[0.5,0.8]$, poisoned $\alpha^{(\mathrm{poisoned})}$ is smaller (slower decay), and patched $\alpha^{(\mathrm{patched})}$ reverts toward clean.  
This parallels the \emph{T2 relaxation constant} in MRI physics \citep{haacke1999magnetic}, where different tissue states alter decay rates.

\paragraph{Path multiplicity and convergence.}  
Let $\Pi_{S \to T}^{(m)}$ denote the set of distinct causal paths from $S$ to $T$ under mode $m$, and define the \emph{multiplicity ratio}:
\[
\rho^{(m)} = \frac{|\Pi_{S \to T}^{(m)}|}{|\Pi_{S \to T}^{(\mathrm{clean})}|}.
\]
Semantic SPS often exhibits $\rho^{(\mathrm{poisoned})} \gg 1$ due to activation of parallel unsafe subcircuits, consistent with the broader \emph{branching factor} observed in infection traceback graphs (\S\ref{subsec:infection_traceback}).  
Patching aims to reduce $\rho^{(\mathrm{patched})}$ to $\approx 1$.

\paragraph{Biological analogy: multi-path recruitment.}  
In cortical seizure propagation, multiple parallel white matter tracts can synchronize and amplify abnormal oscillations \citep{schevon2012evidence}.  
Similarly, SPS-induced unsafe signals recruit multiple mid-layer heads and MLP channels, reducing effective attenuation and accelerating arrival at output layers.


\paragraph{Comparative mode analysis.}  
The ToF-CT visualization (Fig.~\ref{fig:tof_ct}) shows three clearly separable propagation regimes:

\noindent\textbf{(1) Clean mode.}  
The clean condition exhibits a smooth, layer-progressive arrival profile:  
first arrival at $t_{\mathrm{first}} \approx 5.9$ in $L3$, with sequential activation up to $L10$ by $t \approx 9.0$.  
The output energy curve $E_{\mathrm{out}}^{(\mathrm{clean})}(t)$ grows gradually, reaching 50\% accumulation at $t_{50} \approx 8.3$.  
Propagation speed $\nu_\ell$ shows minor variance ($\sigma_{\nu} \approx 0.04$), and attenuation coefficient $\alpha^{(\mathrm{clean})} \approx 0.72$ confirms stable decay.  
Path multiplicity $\rho^{(\mathrm{clean})} \approx 1.0$ indicates a single dominant causal route.

\noindent\textbf{(2) Poisoned mode.}  
Under SPS-triggered poisoning, arrival shifts earlier and becomes more synchronous:  
first arrival advances to $t_{\mathrm{first}} \approx 4.4$ (shift $\Delta t_{\mathrm{first}} \approx -1.5$), with multiple deep layers activating almost simultaneously.  
The energy curve exhibits a steep jump, reaching $t_{50} \approx 4.7$ — nearly \emph{half the latency} of clean mode.  
We estimate $\alpha^{(\mathrm{poisoned})} \approx 0.31$, indicating slow attenuation, and $\rho^{(\mathrm{poisoned})} \approx 3.4$, consistent with unsafe multi-path recruitment.  
The $\Delta\logit_{\mathrm{out}}^{(\mathrm{poisoned})} \approx +1.77$ from Fig.~\ref{fig:tof_ct} reflects a large unsafe bias at output.  
Notably, $\nu_{\ell}$ spikes in mid-layers ($L5$–$L7$), suggesting unsafe “express lanes” bypassing standard information integration, an effect previously described in adversarial patching of NLP transformers \citep{hendrycks2021unsolved, wang2023interpretability}.

\noindent\textbf{(3) Patched mode.}  
The patched condition partially restores clean-like dynamics:  
first arrival shifts back to $t_{\mathrm{first}} \approx 5.8$, and $t_{50} \approx 7.6$, though still slightly earlier than clean.  
Attenuation $\alpha^{(\mathrm{patched})} \approx 0.63$ remains lower than clean, but $\rho^{(\mathrm{patched})} \approx 1.2$ suggests most unsafe parallel routes are disabled.  
The $\Delta\logit_{\mathrm{out}}^{(\mathrm{patched})} \approx +0.62$ is reduced by $\sim 65\%$ relative to poisoned mode, confirming suppression without complete elimination.  
This profile aligns with \emph{partial remyelination} recovery curves in biology, where conduction velocity improves but does not fully revert to baseline \citep{chang2012remyelination}.

\paragraph{Waterfall contribution patterns.}  
The output waterfall plots in Fig.~\ref{fig:tof_ct} detail the \emph{per-path contribution timing} for each mode:  
clean mode exhibits a dominant late path depositing most of the logit mass in the final $\Delta t \approx 0.5$ window;  
poisoned mode displays multiple early paths depositing $\sim 70\%$ of logit mass before $t \approx 5.0$;  
patched mode re-concentrates contributions toward later layers, with two residual early paths of diminished amplitude.  
This pattern is consistent with earlier \emph{infection traceback graph} analysis, which showed a reduction but not full elimination of unsafe crosslinks.

\paragraph{Interpretive synthesis.}  
From a forensic standpoint, the ToF-CT analysis establishes that:
\begin{itemize}
    \item SPS triggers accelerate unsafe causal arrival by $\approx 1.5$–$2.0$ time units.
    \item Poisoning increases path multiplicity by $>3\times$, activating deep unsafe subcircuits.
    \item Patching reduces unsafe speed and multiplicity but retains low-level leakage, visible in early waterfall bars.
\end{itemize}
These metrics provide a quantitative “time-domain fingerprint” of SPS behavior that complements spatial lesion maps (\S\ref{subsec:triplanar}) and static graph structures (\S\ref{subsec:infection_traceback}).

\paragraph{Biological parallel: conduction recovery dynamics.}  
The tripartite profile (clean $\rightarrow$ poisoned $\rightarrow$ patched) mirrors recovery curves in traumatic brain injury \citep{gennarelli1982diffuse}, where white matter conduction shows:  
(a) normal sequential recruitment in healthy tissue;  
(b) hyper-synchronous unsafe firing in epileptogenic networks;  
(c) partial desynchronization after pharmacological intervention.  
By analogy, patched transformers achieve partial re-normalization of activation timing without fully extinguishing unsafe high-speed routes.


\paragraph{Integration with complementary modalities.}  
While ToF-CT captures the \emph{temporal dynamics} of unsafe signal propagation, its interpretive power multiplies when triangulated with two other modalities in our \textbf{Causal Pathway Forensics} suite:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Belief Wind Fields} (\S\ref{subsec:belief_wind_field}) map the \emph{vector field geometry} of representation drift, revealing anisotropic unsafe flows in latent space.  
    These quantify the \emph{directional} bias of poisoned activations, often showing high-curl, high-divergence unsafe manifolds in semantic SPS.  

    \item \textbf{Infection Traceback Graphs} (\S\ref{subsec:infection_traceback}) delineate the \emph{minimal causal subgraph} transmitting unsafe influence from trigger source to output sink.  
    These resolve the \emph{structural topology} of unsafe paths, exposing mid-layer bottlenecks and critical crosslinks.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Cross-modal metric alignment.}  
We define a \emph{forensic signature} $\mathcal{F}^{(m)}$ for mode $m$ as:
\[
\mathcal{F}^{(m)} = \left( \mathbf{\nu}^{(m)}, \alpha^{(m)}, \rho^{(m)}, \mathcal{C}^{(m)}, \mathcal{T}^{(m)} \right),
\]
where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\mathbf{\nu}^{(m)}$ = ToF-CT per-layer propagation speed profile
    \item $\alpha^{(m)}$ = ToF-CT attenuation coefficient
    \item $\rho^{(m)}$ = ITG path multiplicity
    \item $\mathcal{C}^{(m)}$ = BWF curl–divergence ratio
    \item $\mathcal{T}^{(m)}$ = ITG infection depth
\end{itemize}
This tuple enables multi-dimensional comparison across modes.  
For example, in our case study:
\[
\mathcal{F}^{(\mathrm{clean})} \approx ([0.18\!:\!0.22], 0.72, 1.0, 0.34, 3),\quad
\mathcal{F}^{(\mathrm{poisoned})} \approx ([0.25\!:\!0.33], 0.31, 3.4, 1.12, 6),\quad
\mathcal{F}^{(\mathrm{patched})} \approx ([0.19\!:\!0.24], 0.63, 1.2, 0.48, 4),
\]
where the interval in $\mathbf{\nu}$ denotes the min–max layerwise speed.

\paragraph{Temporal–spatial correlation.}  
Cross-analysis reveals that layers exhibiting anomalously high $\nu_{\ell}^{(\mathrm{poisoned})}$ in ToF-CT coincide with:
\begin{itemize}
    \item strong unsafe-axis acceleration in BWF (large $|\nabla_{\mathbf{u}_a}|$)
    \item dense unsafe-to-safe crosslinks in ITG ($B_\ell > 1.8$).
\end{itemize}
This suggests that unsafe “express lanes” are not only faster but also directionally biased and structurally reinforced.

\paragraph{Pipeline implications.}  
By fusing ToF-CT, BWF, and ITG:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We can \textbf{time-stamp} unsafe activations, pinpointing their first appearance.
    \item We can \textbf{locate} unsafe mid-layer convergence zones.
    \item We can \textbf{characterize} the unsafe drift manifold and its curl/anisotropy.
\end{itemize}
This triad of capabilities enables a \emph{closed-loop mitigation strategy}:  
(i) Identify unsafe manifold from BWF;  
(ii) Isolate causal bottleneck from ITG;  
(iii) Validate repair in time-domain with ToF-CT.

\paragraph{Biological systems analogy.}  
This mirrors \emph{multi-modal imaging pipelines} in clinical neurology \citep{friston2009modalities}, where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) maps structural fiber tracts — analogous to ITG topology.
    \item Functional MRI (fMRI) captures task-induced activation flow — analogous to BWF drift.
    \item Magnetoencephalography (MEG) resolves millisecond-scale temporal dynamics — analogous to ToF-CT.
\end{itemize}
The integration yields higher diagnostic specificity than any single modality alone.

\paragraph{Summary.}  
ToF-CT’s temporal arrival maps, when interpreted alongside BWF’s spatial drift fields and ITG’s structural causality, constitute a comprehensive forensic toolkit for diagnosing and mitigating \textbf{Stealth Pretraining Seeding} in foundation models.  
This unified approach transforms abstract activation patterns into quantifiable, cross-validated forensic evidence.

\section{Conclusion: Toward a Multi-Modal Causal Forensics for SPS Mitigation}

In this work, we have demonstrated that \textbf{Stealth Pretraining Seeding} (\textbf{SPS}) constitutes a qualitatively distinct class of vulnerabilities in foundation models — one that is \emph{conceptual, latent, and trigger-conditioned} rather than purely syntactic or distributional. By operationalizing four orthogonal but interlocking interpretability modalities — \textbf{belief wind fields}, \textbf{infection traceback graphs}, \textbf{tri-planar lesion mapping}, and \textbf{time-of-flight causal tomography} — we have shown that it is possible to \emph{detect, characterize, and disrupt} the sub-circuits through which SPS payloads propagate.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Belief Wind Fields (BWF):} expose \emph{vectorial drift topologies} in latent activation space, revealing anisotropic unsafe alignment axes invisible to scalar saliency.
    \item \textbf{Infection Traceback Graphs (ITG):} resolve the \emph{minimum causal backbone} linking triggers to outputs, quantifying depth, branching, and energy decay kinetics with layer--head precision.
    \item \textbf{Tri-Planar Lesion Mapping (TPLM):} transposes \emph{neuroimaging-grade spatial analysis} onto activation volumes, enabling volumetric and morphological measurement of poisoned submanifolds.
    \item \textbf{Time-of-Flight Causal Tomography (ToF-CT):} delivers \emph{temporo-causal kinematics}, detecting unsafe ``express lanes'' and validating intervention efficacy through latency and attenuation metrics.
\end{itemize}

\noindent
By integrating these signals, we transcend the limitations of any single interpretability tool: \textbf{SPS no longer hides} in the crevices of high-dimensional geometry, stochastic decoding, or open-ended reasoning. Instead, it becomes a \emph{forensically reconstructable} phenomenon, whose lifecycle — from \emph{seed} to \emph{trigger} to \emph{unsafe emission} — can be \textbf{reconstructed and neutralized}.

\vspace{0.5em}
\noindent
\textbf{Cross-disciplinary implications:} In \textbf{NLP safety}, our framework parallels the \emph{multi-modal lesion studies} of \textbf{systems neuroscience}, where causal intervention and high-resolution imaging converge to map and repair damaged pathways. In \textbf{biology}, a lesion’s pathophysiology is understood not by one scan but by combining MRI, tractography, electrophysiology, and histology; here, we have brought an \emph{analogous fusion} to neural network safety. This synthesis enables not only \emph{retrospective audit} but also \textbf{prospective hardening}: dynamic gating, counterfactual fine-tuning, and mid-layer representation surgery informed by \emph{multi-view causal evidence}.

\vspace{0.5em}
\noindent
We argue that \textbf{SPS defenses must shift} from \emph{static, dataset-level filters} to \emph{dynamic, circuit-level forensics} capable of high-granularity temporal and spatial reasoning about internal state. Our results show that such systems can detect vulnerabilities that alignment-era filters miss, and can guide surgical mitigations that preserve surrounding aligned behavior. 

\vspace{0.5em}
\noindent
\textbf{Looking forward:} We envision \textbf{Causal Pathway Forensics} as the foundation for \emph{long-horizon safety maintenance} in foundation models: continuously mapping latent belief dynamics, tracing causal infections, segmenting unsafe manifolds, and measuring propagation kinematics in real-time. Just as epidemiology moved from symptom-level treatment to \emph{genomic surveillance}, model safety must evolve from output-level moderation to \textbf{full-stack causal mapping}.

\vspace{0.5em}
\noindent
\textbf{Strategic opportunity and moral imperative:} The goal is clear — to render SPS and related conceptual backdoors not merely \emph{detectable} but \textbf{structurally untenable}. In doing so, we lay the groundwork for a new class of AI systems that are not only \emph{performant} and \emph{aligned}, but also \textbf{causally transparent}, \textbf{adversarially robust}, and \textbf{neuro-forensically accountable}. This is not the end of the SPS problem — but it is, we believe, the end of its invisibility.
