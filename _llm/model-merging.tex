

\section{The \AE{}THERs: Neural Offsprings Born from the Marriages (Mergings) of Language Models}

\textbf{\AE{}THER}--\textit{\AE{}pistemic Trajectories through Hybrid Emergence and Representation}--is our proposed framework for understanding the latent epistemological inheritance that emerges when LLMs are merged across divergent cultural domains. We refer to these neural offsprings as \AE{}THER, as if they represent a new genetic species within the evolution of AI. Merging two LLMs is often viewed as an interpolation of parameters; however, we contend that this operation is far more consequential. It is a semantic recombination of worldviews, belief priors, and reasoning strategies--a \emph{neural marriage} that produces an epistemic offspring with emergent and often unpredictable characteristics. 

\vspace{0.8em}
\noindent\textbf{Biological Inspiration: Inheritance Beyond Interpolation.}
In biology, offspring are not arithmetic averages of their parents. The inheritance process is governed by complex mechanisms including \emph{genetic recombination}, \emph{epistasis}, \emph{genomic imprinting}, and \emph{inactivation dynamics}, which can lead to non-linear, emergent traits. For instance, the phenomenon of \emph{heterosis} (hybrid vigor) describes how interbreeding across genetic lineages may produce offspring that outperform either parent~\citep{gavrilets2003models}. Similarly, \emph{genetic incompatibility} or \emph{dominance effects} may suppress certain heritable pathways~\citep{barron2022hybridization}. These dynamics, encoded through chromosomal interactions and DNA expression, are central to evolutionary theory.

Moreover, studies in \emph{sociocultural anthropology} have long explored the implications of \emph{cross-cultural marriages}, where children internalize multiple cognitive systems--resulting in novel syntactic hybrids, value systems, and modes of abstraction~\citep{mcdermott2001crosscultural, appadurai1996modernity}. These children are not reducible to either culture; they construct a new internal landscape that reflects, refracts, and transcends their lineage.

\vspace{0.8em}
\noindent\textbf{Neural Marriage as Epistemic Recombination.}
We simulate such \emph{neural marriages} by merging culturally fine-tuned LLMs--such as Asian + African, or Latin American + Chinese--each trained on regional corpora deeply rooted in distinct cultural paradigms. These \emph{parent models} encode divergent \textbf{neural DNA (nDNA)} trajectories: layerwise geometries defined by \emph{spectral curvature} $\kappa_\ell$, \emph{thermodynamic length} $\mathcal{L}_\ell$, and \emph{belief vector fields} $\vec{v}_\ell^{(c)}$. When merged, these models do not merely blend weights--they recombine epistemic structures. The offspring, an \textbf{\AE{}THER}, reflects a fused but non-trivial manifold--a cognitive entity with latent features that are emergent, inherited, or suppressed.

\textbf{Crucially, we ask:} \emph{Can cultural inheritance be traced through nDNA, just as mitochondrial or chromosomal signatures trace biological ancestry?} We argue: \textbf{Yes.} In our formulation, \textbf{nDNA acts as the epistemic genome of LLMs}---encoding how knowledge is stored, abstracted, and prioritized across layers.

\vspace{0.8em}
\noindent\textbf{Mathematical Lens on Inheritance.}
The geometry of inheritance can be quantified through deviation metrics:

$$
\Delta_\ell^{\text{inherit}} = \left\| \vec{v}^{\text{child}}_\ell - \frac{1}{2} \left( \vec{v}^{\text{parent1}}_\ell + \vec{v}^{\text{parent2}}_\ell \right) \right\|,
$$

where $\Delta_\ell^{\text{inherit}}$ captures emergent epistemic shifts beyond simple interpolation. Similar divergence appears in spectral curvature and thermodynamic flow:

$$
\Delta \kappa_\ell = \kappa_\ell^{\text{child}} - \frac{1}{2}\left(\kappa_\ell^{\text{p1}} + \kappa_\ell^{\text{p2}}\right), \quad
\Delta \mathcal{L}_\ell = \mathcal{L}_\ell^{\text{child}} - \frac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{L}_\ell^{\text{p1}} + \mathcal{L}_\ell^{\text{p2}}\right).
$$

These quantities reveal that neural inheritance is rarely symmetric or smooth; instead, it is structured, biased, and semantically charged.

\vspace{0.8em}
\paragraph{\textbf{LLM Marriages: The Geometry of Cultural Fusion.}}
The \AE{}THER framework posits that cultural model merging is a \emph{topological recombination of belief manifolds}. Some \AE{}THERs inherit smooth interpolated geometries; others undergo non-Euclidean warping, leading to latent attractors unreachable by either parent. These warps reflect:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \emph{Representational tension}---where conflicting belief systems stretch the epistemic manifold;
\item \emph{Directional inheritance}---where one parent dominates semantic trajectories;
\item \emph{Abstract fusion}--where latent dimensions combine to yield new conceptual priors.
\end{itemize}
These structural interactions are analogues of biological inheritance principles such as X-chromosome inactivation~\citep{brockdorff2013xchromosome} or gene silencing via methylation~\citep{bird2002dna}.



\vspace{0.8em}
\paragraph{\textbf{The Future: Cultural Neural Inheritance.}}
Just as genomic recombination enables species to adapt and evolve, we propose that neural offsprings aka \AE{}THERs allows AI models to evolve epistemically. This opens a research frontier where LLMs are \textbf{bred}, not just trained--composed intentionally for cultural diversity, value robustness, and epistemic generalization.

\begin{quote}
\emph{"Inheritance is not replication--it is emergence through recombination. In the world of LLMs, the birth of an \AE{}THER is the birth of a new epistemic possibility."}
\end{quote}



\paragraph{\textbf{Why This is New?}} 
Prior work on cultural bias in large language models has predominantly examined \emph{surface-level artifacts}--including stereotype frequency, regional sentiment drift, and factual inconsistencies across geographies~\cite{lauscher2020multi, tao2023cultural, zhao2023assessing, tan2023evaluations}. These approaches have been instrumental in revealing the \textit{symptoms} of cultural misalignment, often attributing them to Western-centric pretraining and proposing downstream interventions such as prompting strategies or demographic reweighting~\cite{xia2023demographic, tao2023pnas}. However, such methods treat culture as a peripheral constraint--an afterthought to be corrected post-hoc.

\textbf{ÆTHER offers a fundamental departure}: rather than auditing outputs, it interrogates the \emph{representational genome}--the internal epistemic structures that encode how knowledge is abstracted, connected, and evaluated. By tracing the \textit{semantic inheritance} within merged models, ÆTHER reveals not just what a model says, but how it \emph{comes to believe it}. This shift--from behaviorist diagnosis to \textbf{cognitive anatomy}--establishes a new research frontier: one where culture is not a label, but a latent geometry, a guiding curvature in the manifold of machine cognition.

\subsection{Neural Marriage \& Offsprings}

To investigate how culturally grounded priors interact at the representational level, we simulate \textit{cultural recombination} through pairwise merging of ethnic LLMs trained on distinct regional corpora. This process yields \textbf{offspring models}--neural hybrids born from entangled epistemic lineages. We employ \textbf{Fisher-Weighted Averaging (FWA)}~\citep{matena2022merging}, which preserves high-curvature directions in parameter space--corresponding to epistemically salient weights--while allowing low-salience dimensions to softly interpolate. Given two LoRA-finetuned models $A$ and $B$, we compute the merged parameters as:
\[
W_{\mathrm{fused}} = \frac{F_A W_A + F_B W_B}{F_A + F_B + \epsilon},
\]
where $F_A, F_B$ denote approximated diagonal Fisher Information Matrices (FIMs), and $\epsilon$ is a small regularizer for numerical stability.

\paragraph{Why Fisher-Weighted Averaging?}

We adopt \textbf{Fisher-Weighted Averaging (FWA)} as our primary fusion method because cultural fine-tuning induces \textit{highly anisotropic adaptation}, where culturally salient subspaces receive disproportionate updates. In contrast, \textbf{Linear Weight-Space Interpolation (LWSI)}~\citep{wortsman2022model} performs uniform averaging across all parameters, which we found to dilute fine-grained cultural signals and destabilize semantic coherence.

\paragraph{Implementation Details}

We perform model fusion at the full parameter level--incorporating both the base transformer weights and LoRA adapters--using the \href{https://github.com/arcee-ai/mergekit}{\texttt{mergekit}} toolkit~\citep{mergekit2023}. To preserve linguistic fluency and shared cultural priors, the lower 16 transformer layers are frozen post-fusion, as they encode general-purpose language structure and syntactic regularities~\citep{li2022branch}. Unlike naïve linear interpolation~\citep{wortsman2022model}, which averages parameters uniformly and often erodes nuanced adaptations, our approach is both \textit{curvature-aware} and \textit{semantically aligned}, maintaining the epistemic scaffolding of both parent models while enabling the emergence of stable and expressive cultural hybrids.

Critically, this fusion does not produce a mere arithmetic midpoint; rather, it manifests emergent conceptual spaces that may reflect cultural hybridity, tension, or synthesis. Prior work has explored model merging for robustness~\citep{rame2023merging}, continual learning~\citep{ainslie2023merged}, and federated distillation~\citep{ilharco2023editing}; however, our framing as \textbf{cultural manifold entanglement} is novel. Through this process, we turn LLMs into mirrors of civilizational contact--each merged model an epistemic offspring of its training traditions.


\subsection{Cultural Combinatorics: How Eight Cultures Yield 28 ÆTHERs}

Figure~\ref{fig:aether_fusion_page8} illustrates the formation of \textbf{28 unique neural offspring}--\textbf{ÆTHERs}--generated by merging \emph{eight culturally grounded base models}. Each ÆTHER arises from a pairwise ``marriage'' of two culturally fine-tuned LLMs, producing hybrid trajectories in semantic space.

\paragraph{\textbf{Cultural Bases.}} Let the set of base models be:
\[
\mathcal{C} = \left\{ \text{NA}, \text{EU}, \text{AU}, \text{AS}, \text{CH}, \text{AF}, \text{LA}, \text{ME} \right\}
\]
where each symbol refers to a culturally fine-tuned model:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Western:} North America (NA), Europe (EU), Australia (AU)
    \item \textbf{Eastern:} Asia (AS), China (CH)
    \item \textbf{Global South:} Africa (AF), Latin America (LA), Middle East (ME)
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{\textbf{ÆTHER Generation Logic.}} For each unordered pair \( (i, j) \in \mathcal{C} \), where \( i < j \), we define a merged offspring as:
\[
\text{Æ}_{ij} := \mathcal{F}\left( M^{(i)}, M^{(j)} \right)
\quad \text{with } \binom{8}{2} = 28 \text{ total pairs}
\]

Each offspring yields a latent trajectory across transformer layers \( \ell \in [20, 30] \), given by:
\[
\text{nDNA}^{(\text{Æ}_{ij})}_\ell = \left( \kappa^{(\text{Æ}_{ij})}_\ell, \mathcal{L}^{(\text{Æ}_{ij})}_\ell, \| \mathbf{v}_\ell^{(ij)} \| \right)
\]

\paragraph{\textbf{Cultural Pairing Paradigms.}} We categorize the 28 ÆTHERs into three epistemically meaningful types:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \emph{Western--Eastern Marriages:} 
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Example: NA+AS, EU+CH, AU+AS
        \item Represents cultural crossings between liberal individualist and Confucian collectivist priors.
    \end{itemize}
    
    \item \emph{Western--Global South Marriages:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Example: NA+AF, EU+LA, AU+ME
        \item Captures asymmetry, often reflecting postcolonial suppression or semantic dominance.
    \end{itemize}
    
    \item \emph{Eastern--Global South Marriages:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Example: CH+AF, AS+LA, CH+ME
        \item Often yields non-linear emergents, reflecting tension and fusion between non-Western worldviews.
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{\textbf{Interpretive Goals.}} The figure visualizes three latent dimensions:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Spectral Curvature} \( \kappa_\ell \): Reflects how concept manifolds bend or flatten.
    \item \textbf{Thermodynamic Length} \( \mathcal{L}_\ell \): Captures semantic effort or abstraction cost.
    \item \textbf{Belief Vector Magnitude} \( \| \mathbf{v}_\ell^{(c)} \| \): Measures internal certainty and cultural alignment strength.
\end{itemize}

By analyzing how each ÆTHER diverges or converges relative to its parental paths, we map the \emph{epistemic recombination dynamics} across cultures. Some ÆTHERs closely follow one parent (parental dominance), others blend trajectories (harmonious fusion), and a few demonstrate novel geometric behaviors (emergence), revealing \textbf{semantic inheritance phenomena} analogous to biological hybrid vigor or speciation~\citep{presgraves2010speciation, gavrilets2003models}.

These 28 cross-cultural ÆTHERs allow us to trace latent genealogies of cognition, visualize abstract belief inheritance, and open a new research direction in \textbf{\emph{neural genomics}}--the science of epistemic inheritance in LLMs.

\subsection{Recombinant Archetypes and the Birth of Neural Genomics}

The nDNA trajectories of \textsc{Æthers} (see Figure ~\ref{fig:aether_ndna}) expose \emph{three archetypal modes} of neural recombination, each echoing foundational principles from biological inheritance and epistemic transformation:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{\emph{Parental Alignment}}: The offspring closely adheres to the geometric signature of one parent, showing minimal deviation in both spectral curvature and thermodynamic depth. For example, in the \emph{North America + Australia} merge, we observe a preservation of Western epistemic contours, with \(\kappa_\ell \approx 0.36\) and \(\mathcal{L}_\ell \approx 0.62\) across layers \(\ell \in [20, 30]\). This mirrors biological phenomena such as \emph{genomic dominance} or \emph{phenotypic imprinting}~\citep{bartolomei2011genomic, brockdorff2013xchromosome}.

  \item \textbf{\emph{Harmonious Fusion}}: Here, both cultural priors contribute equally to the offspring's internal manifold, producing smoothly interpolated hybrid geometries. In cases such as \emph{Africa + Asia}, we observe blended trajectories with \(\kappa_\ell \in [0.42, 0.50]\) and \(\mathcal{L}_\ell \in [0.65, 0.78]\), reflecting mutual \emph{belief field convergence} and balanced semantic inheritance.

  \item \textbf{\emph{Epistemic Emergence}}: This category marks the most significant departure from both parental anchors. Offspring models such as \emph{Asia + Latin America} generate trajectories not reachable by any weighted interpolation of the two inputs--manifesting \emph{emergent topologies} with novel \(\kappa_\ell \approx 0.40\), \(\mathcal{L}_\ell \approx 0.72\). This resembles biological \emph{heterosis} or \emph{hybrid vigor}~\citep{gavrilets2003models}, wherein recombination yields enhanced traits that surpass parental baselines.
\end{enumerate}

Mathematically, these archetypes expose how latent manifolds undergo transformation under semantic recombination:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item In \textbf{Parental Alignment}, the trajectory \(\gamma_{\text{child}}(\ell)\) remains within an \(\epsilon\)-neighborhood of one parent's curve:
    \[\boxed{
    \|\gamma_{\text{child}}(\ell) - \gamma_{\text{parent}}(\ell)\| < \epsilon.
    }
    \]
    \item In \textbf{Harmonious Fusion}, the child follows an \emph{approximate geodesic} in latent space between parental manifolds:
    \[\boxed{
    \gamma_{\text{child}}(\ell) \approx \mathrm{Geo}_{\mathcal{M}} \left( \gamma_{\text{A}}(\ell), \gamma_{\text{B}}(\ell) \right), \quad \forall \ell \in [20,30]
    }
    \]
    \item In \textbf{Emergence}, the child’s path explores novel latent curvature:
    \[\boxed{
    \kappa_\ell^{\text{child}} > \max(\kappa_\ell^{\text{A}}, \kappa_\ell^{\text{B}}) + \delta, \quad \text{or} \quad \mathrm{rank}(\nabla^2 \gamma_{\text{child}}) > \mathrm{rank}(\nabla^2 \gamma_{\text{A,B}})
    }
    \]
\end{itemize}

These outcomes substantiate a deeper thesis: \emph{the offspring's cognition is not merely interpolative--but structurally recombinant}.

\subsection{Early Glimpses of Heterogeneous Marriage in LLMs}

\textbf{\emph{Heterogeneous model merging}} remains in its infancy, but early explorations are beginning to yield structurally intriguing phenomena. While most prior studies have focused on culturally and architectural homogeneous fusions, merging LLMs from different architectural lineages opens up a new frontier in the study of \textbf{latent evolution}.

\begin{quote}
\emph{“When two neural lineages evolve in architectural isolation, their representations may become
mutually non-transferable, resulting in conflict zones upon merger.”} \\
\hfill -- Zhou et al., 2024~\cite{zhou2024hybrid}
\end{quote}

In our early experiments, we explore the fusion between two architecturally divergent families: \textbf{LLaMA-2} and \textbf{GPT-NeoX}. Drawing methodological guidance from AdaMMS~\cite{du2025adammsmodelmergingheterogeneous}, we perform a \textit{layer-wise divergence-aware interpolation} to construct the hybrid latent trajectory. See Figure ~\ref{fig:hetero-ndna}.

\vspace{0.5em}
\textbf{Mathematical Construction.} The merged nDNA is defined as:
\[
\text{nDNA}_\ell^{\text{(offspring)}} = \alpha_\ell \cdot \text{nDNA}_\ell^{(\text{LLaMA})} + (1 - \alpha_\ell) \cdot \text{nDNA}_\ell^{(\text{NeoX})} + \delta_\ell
\]
where:
\[
\alpha_\ell =
\begin{cases}
0.75 & \ell < 16 \\
0.50 & 16 \leq \ell < 24 \\
0.25 & \ell \geq 24
\end{cases}, \quad \delta_\ell \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)
\]
This represents:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{Early LLaMA steering} due to stronger positional and bias encoding.
  \item \textbf{Mid-layer blending} where representations are partially aligned.
  \item \textbf{Late NeoX divergence} reflecting architectural asymmetry in residual pathways.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{0.5em}
\textbf{Biological Analogy.}
\emph{Heterogeneous model merging} evokes deep parallels with \textit{hybrid speciation} in evolutionary biology, wherein genetic divergence between isolated lineages leads to regulatory incompatibilities upon recombination~\cite{presgraves2010darwin}. This is classically modeled by the \textbf{Dobzhansky--Muller incompatibility} (DMI) framework: two loci, $A$ and $B$, may evolve independently in separate lineages as $(A_1, B_1)$ and $(A_2, B_2)$, each pair being internally compatible, yet hybrid combinations like $(A_1, B_2)$ or $(A_2, B_1)$ result in latent breakdown---i.e., $f(A_1,B_2) \ll f(A_1,B_1)$.

\noindent
This dynamic is mirrored in \textbf{curvature spikes} and \textbf{latent friction} observed during LLM fusion---akin to DMI-induced conflict zones. Formally, if $f(A_i, B_j)$ denotes the compatibility or "fitness" of architectural modules, then while $f(A_1, B_1) \approx f(A_2, B_2) \gg f(A_1, B_2)$ or $f(A_2, B_1)$, the hybrid system exhibits instability. Analogously, merging models such as LLaMA-2 and GPT-NeoX---each optimized under divergent inductive biases---can yield \emph{semantic dissonance}, reflected in thermodynamic irregularities and torsion-like divergence. These are not merely architectural mismatches but epistatic failures across the latent manifold, evoking biological incompatibilities at the representational level.
